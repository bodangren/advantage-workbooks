## 1. How to use a brief

1. Copy the brief's front-matter block into a new file `origins-3.2-drafts/pNN-<title>.md`.
2. Write three paragraphs from the text plan. The plan gives the story and some model lines. The writer can change the lines.
3. Run the text check (task C3). Fix every FAIL. A WARN on question marks is acceptable in 4 lessons or less. A WARN on new words is acceptable (plan §4 priority rule: a good story comes first).
4. Daniel approves the text on the review page (Oct 3–7).

A brief can change up to two glossed words (plan §5). Record every change in §5 of this file.

## 2. Rules for every lesson

These rules come from the plan §4 and the series bible. The text check tests the numbered ones.

1. 150–200 words in 3 paragraphs.
2. Mean sentence length 5.0–5.5 words; no sentence longer than 10 words. Origins 3.1 averages 4.2, so write about one sentence in three with 7–10 words.
3. 95% or more of the running words are on the Starters list. Names, the glossed words, the words on the brief's allow list, and "Woof" are not counted.
4. All 12 glossed words occur in the text. Aim for 6 or more new Starters words (the check shows the list); fewer gives a WARN, not a FAIL.
5. 4 or more glossed words from earlier lessons occur again. The brief's recycle list gives the ones to use.
6. Numbers are words ("thirteen"), times are full hours ("seven o'clock"), and spelling is American ("color", "favorite", "Mom").
7. Two or more question marks (book rule: in 10 or more of the 14 lessons). Dialogue in double quotation marks (book rule: 6 or more lessons).
- Vary the first line. Only a few lessons start with "This is …".
- End with one short line with a feeling or a value.
- End every line of a sign, a chant, or a list with `.`, `!` or `?`.
- Pip is a small brown puppy with a red collar. He lives with Tom and Lily. He says "Woof!" and does not talk.

### P01 — Hello! I Am May

| | |
|---|---|
| Text type | Functional: a self-introduction in class, with questions and answers |
| Cast and place | May (new girl, 7), Teacher Kim, Lily, the class; Lily's classroom. Pat (May's brother, 5) is only mentioned. |
| Objectives | **L19.1** May gives her name, age, and where she lives. **L20.1** The class asks her "What is your name?", "How old are you?", "Where do you live?". **R17.1** Sentences that introduce May and Pat. **R10.2** Three or more questions. **L13.2, R18.3** "Hi", "Hello", and "Goodbye" all occur. **L11.1** "Thank you, May." **L12.1** A yes/no answer ("No, he is not!"). |
| Glossed (12) | hi, spell, English, brother, sister, baby, live, classmate, goodbye, nine, ten, school. **New (6):** hi, spell, English, baby, classmate, goodbye |
| Recycle | name, old, house, favorite (all glossed in E12) |
| Grammar | *What is your name? How old are you? Where do you live? Have you got a …? How do you spell …? I live …* |
| Book rules | 3+ question marks; dialogue |

**Text plan**
1. Open with Teacher Kim: "Good morning, class! This is May. She is new." May: "Hi! Hello, class!" A classmate asks her name. "How do you spell May?" "M-A-Y." (The check lists the letters as guessed names. That is expected.)
2. The questions go on. "How old are you?" "I am seven." "Where do you live?" "I live in a white house next to the school." "Have you got a brother or a sister?" "I have got a brother, Pat." Leo: "Is he a baby?" "No! He is five!"
3. "What is your favorite lesson?" "English!" Teacher Kim: "Thank you, May." Now the class has ten girls and nine boys. May is Lily's new classmate, and she sits next to Lily. At the end of the day: "Goodbye, May!" "Goodbye, Lily!" Closing: May is happy at her new school.

**Questions and tasks**
- MCQ: (1) How old is May? — seven (obj L20.1). (2) Who is Pat? — May's brother (obj R17.1). (3) What is May's favorite lesson? — English (obj L19.1). (4) What do the children say at the end of the day? — "Goodbye!" (obj L13.2).
- Short answer: What is your name? How do you spell it?
- Writing: Write about you. (My name is ___. I am ___. I live ___. I have got ___.)

**Images:** (1) Teacher Kim and May in front of the class, Thai school uniform. (2) May speaking; a picture bubble of Pat and her white house. (3) Lily and May wave goodbye at the classroom door.

**Continuity:** First appearance of May and Teacher Kim. May lives in a white house next to the school (new canon). May is seven here and eight from P03.

### P02 — Where Is Pip?

| | |
|---|---|
| Text type | Story: a search through every room, with questions and answers |
| Cast and place | Tom, Lily, Pip, Mom; Tom and Lily's house |
| Objectives | **L19.2** Things are found from short descriptions ("the big brown sofa"). **L21.2** Questions about where things are and their color. **R10.2** Four or more questions. **L12.1** "No, he isn't." / "Yes!" **R17.2** Home nouns and colors. **R15.1** "sofa" (Thai โซฟา). |
| Glossed (12) | kitchen, bedroom, bathroom, living room, sofa, lamp, clock, under, behind, between, next to, in front of. **New (8):** kitchen, bathroom, sofa, lamp, clock, behind, between, in front of |
| Recycle | brother, sister, favorite, house |
| Grammar | *Where is Pip? Is he in the kitchen? No, he isn't.* Prepositions of place. |
| Book rules | 4+ question marks; dialogue |

**Text plan**
1. Open with a call: "Pip! Where are you?" It is morning. Tom and his sister, Lily, can't find Pip in the house. His favorite red ball is not in his bed. "Is he in the kitchen?" Lily looks under the table. No, he isn't.
2. Lily's brother, Tom, looks in the bathroom, behind the door. Lily looks in her bedroom, under the bed and between the two chairs. "Is he next to the lamp?" "No, he isn't!" The big clock is on the wall. Use each preposition once.
3. Mom comes to the living room. "Look! Behind the big brown sofa!" Pip is there. He is sleeping with his red ball. Tom puts the ball in front of the sofa. Closing: Pip is happy. Tom and Lily are happy too.

**Questions and tasks**
- MCQ: (1) Where is Pip at the end? — behind the sofa (obj L19.2). (2) "Is Pip in the kitchen?" — No, he isn't (obj L12.1). (3) What color is Pip's ball? — red (obj L21.2). (4) Who finds Pip? — Mom.
- Short answer: What is in your living room?
- Writing: Draw a room in your home. Write where three things are. (The lamp is next to the sofa.)

**Images:** (1) Tom and Lily in the kitchen, calling. (2) Lily looking under the bed. (3) Pip asleep behind the brown sofa with his red ball; a lamp and a clock in view.

### P04 — The Counting Chant

| | |
|---|---|
| Text type | Functional: a class chant with actions, numbers one to twenty, inside a short classroom frame |
| Cast and place | Teacher Kim, the class (Lily, May, Leo); the classroom |
| Objectives | **L18.2** Familiar words in a chant. **L21.3** The gist of the chant, with actions. **R12.1, R13.2, L10.2** Number words to ten. **R18.2** Number words to twenty. **R20.2** Plurals (hands, feet). **L15.1** clap, jump. |
| Glossed (12) | clap, count, number, song, jump, hand, foot, eleven, twelve, fifteen, twenty, again. **New (8):** clap, number, song, foot, eleven, twelve, fifteen, twenty |
| Recycle | nine, ten, sing, classmate |
| Grammar | Chant lines with imperatives; plurals; *Let's count!* *How many …?* |
| Book rules | 2+ question marks; dialogue (Teacher Kim) |

**Text plan**
1. Open with the rhythm: "Clap, clap! One, two, three!" Teacher Kim: "Let's sing a number song! Stand next to your desk." She asks: "How many hands have you got?" "Two!" "How many feet?" "Two!"
2. The chant, one line per sentence. Count one to ten with actions, then eleven to twenty. Model lines: "One, two, three, four. Clap your hands with me!" "Eleven, twelve! Jump, jump, jump!" "Fifteen! Show me your two feet!" "Twenty! Clap and jump with me!" Mix short chant lines with longer lines to keep the mean sentence length at 5.0 or more.
3. "Again!" says Lily. The class sings the song again, and Leo claps and jumps at "twenty" (short, funny). Teacher Kim: "Good! Now count your classmates." Closing: The class loves the number song.

**Questions and tasks**
- MCQ: (1) What do the children do at "Eleven, twelve"? — jump (obj L18.2). (2) Which number comes after eleven? — twelve (obj R18.2). (3) How many feet have you got? — two (obj R20.2). (4) What is the song about? — numbers (obj L21.3).
- Short answer: Count your pencils. How many pencils have you got?
- Writing: Write a number chant: ___, ___, ___! Clap your hands! ___, ___, ___! Jump with me!

**Images:** (1) Teacher Kim clapping in front of the class. (2) The chant as a poster, with number words and pictures of hands and feet; the poster text is the article text, letter for letter. (3) The children jumping.

**Audio:** The app needs a chanted recording (rhythm, not plain reading) for L21.3.

### P05 — At the Market

| | |
|---|---|
| Text type | Story: shopping dialogue |
| Cast and place | Grandma, Tom, Pip; a Thai fresh market |
| Objectives | **R18.2, L10.2, R13.2** Number words ("twelve", "thirteen", "six"). **R20.2** Plurals (mangoes, eggs, bananas, lemons). **L11.1** "…, please." "Thank you." **R10.2** "How many …?" **R17.2** Food nouns. **R15.1** apple, lemon (Thai loanwords). |
| Glossed (12) | shop, fruit, mango, banana, coconut, egg, bread, lime, how many, apple, lemon, thirteen. **New (12):** all |
| Recycle | please, thank you, count, twelve |
| Allow | market |
| Grammar | *How many … do you want? … , please. Here you are. Thank you.* |
| Book rules | 2+ question marks; dialogue |

**Text plan**
1. Open: "Grandma has got a big bag." It is morning. Grandma, Tom, and Pip walk to the market. There are a lot of shops. Pip walks next to Tom.
2. At the fruit shop: "How many mangoes do you want?" "Six mangoes, please." Grandma wants bananas, limes, lemons, apples, and one big coconut, too. At the egg shop: "Twelve eggs, please." The woman gives thirteen eggs! Tom counts them: "… eleven, twelve, thirteen!" "Thank you!"
3. Then the bread shop. Grandma gives the bread to Tom, and Tom holds it. "How many bananas have we got?" At home, Grandma makes coconut juice (optional). Closing: Grandma and Tom are happy, and Pip wants the bread!

**Questions and tasks**

- Short answer: How many people are in your family?
- Writing: Write a shopping list. (___ mangoes, ___ eggs, ___ bananas.) Use number words.

**Images:** (1) Grandma, Tom, and Pip at a Thai fresh market. (2) The fruit shop: mangoes, bananas, coconuts, limes, lemons, apples. (3) Tom counting thirteen eggs.
