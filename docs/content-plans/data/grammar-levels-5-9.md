Notes on the sources:
- The Movers and Flyers lists give what is **new** at that level. The learner also knows the list of the level before.
- The A2 Key list is **not** divided by level. It is one inventory. The handbook says that candidates "may have control of only the simplest exponents of the listed forms".
- The 2020 A2 Key handbook (504505) has no grammar inventory. The inventory is in 610842 and in the Key for Schools handbook.
- The graph has **no grammar objectives**. `../mastery-advantage` holds the GSE grammar export (S4) as a source record only. Its levels often differ from Cambridge (see §8). Cambridge decides "allowed". S4 only gives a warning.

Placement rule used here: Movers grammar belongs to level 5 (and the rest of level 4). Flyers grammar belongs to levels 6–7. A2 Key grammar belongs to levels 8–9. Anything that is B1 Preliminary or above is "avoid" at levels 5–9.

## 2. Level 5 (Quest 5, A1) — Movers grammar

**Already used at level 4 (cumulative):** Starters grammar (present simple, present continuous, *can*, *have got*, *there is/are*, *Let's*, *like + -ing*, *would like*, question words, *this/that*), contractions, *Do/Does* questions, ordinal numbers, times, *must* for rules, one or two comparatives with *-er*, *was* in fixed phrases.

**New at level 5 (Movers list, S1 pp. 52–53):**

| Grammar | Example |
|---|---|
| Past simple, regular and irregular, in all four forms | *Tom went to the park. Did Lily see Pip? No, she didn't. We didn't win.* |
| Comparative and superlative adjectives (full set) | *Pip is smaller than Ben's dog. Lily is the fastest.* |
| Comparative and superlative adverbs | *Tom runs more quickly than Sam.* |
| Adverbs of frequency and manner | *Lily never eats fish. Pip barked loudly.* |
| *Must / mustn't*; *have (got) to / had to* | *You mustn't run here. Tom had to clean his room.* |
| *Could* as past of *can* | *Pip could jump over the box.* |
| *Shall I …?* for offers | *Shall I carry your bag, Lily?* |
| Verb + infinitive; *want/ask someone to do*; infinitive of purpose | *Lily wants to go home. Tom asked Ben to help. She went to town to buy a book.* |
| Verb + *-ing* (*went riding*) | *We went swimming on Sunday.* |
| *Because*; *Why …?* | *Why is Tom sad? He is sad because he lost his bag.* |
| Relative clauses with *who, which, where* | *Lily is the girl who has a red hat. This is the shop where Mum works.* |
| *When* clauses (not future) | *When Tom got home, he ate his dinner.* |
| Indirect objects; *What is/was … like?*; *What's the matter?*; *How/What about + noun or -ing?* | *Give it to Pip. What was the weather like? How about going to the park?* |
| *Be called*, *be good at*, *go for a + noun*, *I think/know …* | *A baby dog is called a puppy. Lily is good at drawing. I think he is nice.* |

Load note: this is a large list for one book. Quest 5 should take the past simple, the comparatives, *must/had to*, *could*, *because*, and relative clauses. Quest 6.1 can finish the rest (the plan says Movers ends at level 6).

**Avoid at level 5:** every Flyers form (past continuous, present perfect, *be going to*, *will*, *might*, *may*, *should*, tag questions, conditionals, *before/after* clauses) and everything in A2 Key and above. Keep sentences with one past tense.

## 3. Level 6 (Quest 6.1, 6.2, A1+) — Movers finished, Flyers starts

**Cumulative from earlier levels:** everything in §2 and the level 4 list.

**New at level 6 (Flyers list, S1 pp. 76–77):**

| Grammar | Example |
|---|---|
| Past continuous (background and interrupted action) | *It was raining. Tom was reading when Lily called.* |
| *Be going to* for plans and predictions | *We are going to visit Gran. It isn't going to rain.* |
| *Will / won't* | *Will you help me? I won't be late.* |
| *Might*; *may* for possibility | *Ben might come to the party.* |
| *Should* for advice | *Should we take a coat?* |
| *Could* for suggestions; *shall* for suggestions | *We could ask Mum. Shall we have a picnic?* |
| Conjunction *so* | *Tom was tired, so he went to bed.* |
| Sequence words *first, then, next, finally* (A2 Key list, "Sequence"; see change C6) | *First, mix the milk. Then add the eggs.* |
| *Have you ever …?* (6.2 only, first look) | *Have you ever been to the zoo?* |

**Avoid at level 6:** the present perfect beyond *ever*; all conditionals; *used to*; passive; reported speech; *for/since*.

## 4. Level 7 (Adventure 7.1, 7.2, A2-) — Flyers grammar

**Cumulative from earlier levels:** §2 and §3.

**New at level 7 (Flyers list, S1 pp. 76–77):**

| Grammar | Example |
|---|---|
| Present perfect with *ever, never, just, already, yet* | *Lily has just finished her map. Tom hasn't eaten yet.* |
| Past continuous and past simple with *when* | *I was walking home when I saw Pip.* |
| Zero conditional (*if* + present, present) | *If it is sunny, we swim.* |
| *Before / after* clauses (not future) | *Tom did his homework before he played.* |
| *Where* clauses | *Lily forgot where she put her key.* |
| Tag questions | *That is Ben's bag, isn't it?* |
| *Be / look / sound / feel / taste / smell like*; *make + adjective* | *That sounds like Pip. The smell makes me hungry.* |
| *Be made of*; *What time …?*; *What else/next?*; *See you soon* | *The model is made of wood. See you tomorrow!* |
| Adverb *yet* in negatives | *I haven't called Gran yet.* |

**Avoid at level 7:** first conditional (it is A2 Key, so it waits for level 8); *for/since* with the present perfect; *used to*; passive; reported speech; *too/enough*; *a few/a little* as a taught pattern.

## 5. Level 8 (Adventure 8.1–8.3, A2) — A2 Key grammar begins

**Cumulative from earlier levels:** §2 to §4.

**New at level 8 (A2 Key inventory, S2 pp. 56–57):**

| Grammar | Example |
|---|---|
| Present perfect with *for* and *since* | *Tom has lived here for two years.* |
| First conditional (clause after *if*) | *If it rains, we will stay at home.* |
| *Too* (degree); *a few, a little, many, much, a lot of* | *The bag is too heavy. We have a few apples.* |
| *Needn't*; *don't have to* for no obligation | *You don't have to come. You needn't hurry.* |
| Present continuous with future meaning | *We are meeting Ben at six.* |
| Gerunds as subject and object, and after prepositions; *enjoy / love + -ing* | *Swimming is fun. Lily is good at drawing. Tom enjoys cooking.* |
| *Would* for polite requests | *Would you open the window?* |
| *That* clauses after *know, think, hope, sure* | *I hope you are well. I'm sure it is a bird.* |
| Questions *How much / many / often / long*; *Whose* | *How long did the trip take?* |
| Participles as adjectives; adjective order | *The tired boy slept.* |
| Prepositions of direction and instrument | *We went into the shop. She cut it with scissors.* |
| Comparison with *(not) as … as* (see change C8) | *Pip is not as big as Rex.* |

**Avoid at level 8:** *used to*; passive (wait for level 9); reported speech; past perfect; second conditional; present perfect continuous; *will be able to*.

## 6. Level 9 (Adventure 9.1–9.3, A2+) — A2 Key grammar

**Cumulative from earlier levels:** §2 to §5.

**New at level 9 (A2 Key inventory, S2 pp. 56–57):**

| Grammar | Example |
|---|---|
| Passive, present and past simple, common verbs, no agent | *The bread is made here. The castle was built in 1850. Lily was born in May.* |
| Reporting with *say / tell* + *that* clause | *Mum says that we can go. Ben tells Tom that the shop is shut.* |
| Sequence and cohesion words in longer texts (*first, next, then, after that, finally*; *so, because*) | *After that, we walked home because it was late.* |
| *May* for possibility, with *might* | *It may snow tonight.* |
| Present perfect passive with *just, already, yet* (very rare) | *The room has just been cleaned.* |

**Avoid at level 9 (B1 Preliminary and above):** reported speech with tense change (*She said she had been …*); *used to*; past perfect; second and third conditionals; present perfect continuous; passive of other tenses or with modals; non-defining relative clauses; *was going to*; future continuous; *as soon as*; *wish*; *must/can't* for deduction; *should have*.

## 7. Avoid at all levels 5–9 (B1 and above)

Past perfect; present perfect continuous; second and third conditionals; *used to*; reported speech with tense change; passive in other tenses; non-defining relative clauses; future continuous; *wish / if only*; *should have*; deduction with *must / can't*; *so as to*; *in order to*.

## 9. What I could not confirm

- The A2 Key list has no level order. The split between levels 8 and 9 is a design choice.
- *Enough*, *a little*, and *used to* are not in the Cambridge lists that I read. I used S4 only as a warning.
- I did not find a Cambridge page that says "*said (that)*" is above A2. The B1 label for tense changes comes from S4 only.
- The Starters list pages (S1 pp. 29–30) do not list *was/were*. Quest 4 uses *was* only in fixed phrases.
