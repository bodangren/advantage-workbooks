## 1. Summary

Adventure 9.1 is the first book of level 9 (A2+, `cefr_level = 'A2'`, `ra_level = 9`). It has 14 workbook lessons. Each lesson has 12 glossed words, 2–4 target objectives, and one main grammar point of level 9. QR key: `a9.1/<lesson>`. The book is about school: school-subject texts (science, history, geography, math in daily life), the school news board and the class blog as news, the school nurse's room, two club trips, and one email exchange with a pen pal. It gives the readers their first news report, interview, review, program with a key, recipe with a math task, school science text, and two emails on one topic of level 9.

Text mix: 6 stories and 8 informational or functional texts. The stories are in L02, L04, L05, L06, L09, L14; the other lessons are informational or functional. Every story asks the reader to infer a feeling or a fact that the text does not state (for example, L02: the shop woman gave too much change; L06: Pat ate three green mangoes; L09: the stone says eighteen twenty because the photo of eighteen sixty shows a gate that is forty years old). The book has a traditional story in L05 (*The Frog Under the Shell*, a tale that Grandpa tells at Green Hill). Dates run from Monday, February 1 to Saturday, February 27, 2027, written in one form, month first (*February 1*; in the text, *February first*). Every weekday agrees with the 2027 calendar (February 1, 2027 is a Monday). No lesson falls on a public holiday (section 3, notes). The two club trips are the old town (Saturday, February 13, L09) and the town library map room (Saturday, February 27, L14).

Level 9 uses the A2 Key list on top of the grammar of levels 5–8. The new grammar of level 9 comes in here: the present and past simple passive of common verbs (no agent; not in every lesson), *says that / tells him that* (and *said that* with a simple clause, no tense changes), gerunds as subjects, participles as adjectives, and longer texts with sequence and cohesion words (*first, next, then, after that, finally; so, because*). *May* and *might* show possibility. Each lesson has one main point (section 6). Stories ask the reader to infer feelings and facts that the text does not state; informational texts have headings, a main topic for each paragraph, and facts to scan. Voice follows bible §7: a close third person in the past simple for stories (the present perfect and the first conditional may be in speech); a named writer for a blog post, a diary entry, a review, an interview, or an email (*Posted by Leo*, *Dear Lily, ... Best wishes, Grace*); headings and no "I" in an informational text of the club or the school; a program, a recipe, and a news board speak to the reader. The linking words *and, so, but,* and *because* come in every lesson (bible §7).

On Monday, February 1, May and Lily pin the weekly news board outside the library door, and Teacher Kim checks it before the first bell. Mia asks, "What is the news this week?" ### L03 Where Does Rain Come From?

Text type: functional: a school science text with headings and a diagram, for the class blog. Genre: Science & Space. App type: nonfiction. Place: the classroom (science lesson).

On Wednesday, February 3, Teacher Kim's class studies rain in the science lesson, and Lily writes the notes as "Our Science Page" for the Explorers' Blog. The page has three headings: "The sun heats the sea", "Clouds are made", and "Rain falls". First the sea water is warmed by the sun and goes up into the air; next the air is cooled and small drops are made into clouds; finally the drops become heavy and rain falls. Teacher Kim does a short experiment with a pot of hot water and a cold plate, and drops form on the plate. Mia asks, "Where does the water go after the rain?" and Leo answers, "Back to the sea, along the rivers." Teacher Kim says that it may rain tomorrow and that the same water goes around again and again.

- hero: A classroom. Teacher Kim stands at the whiteboard beside a diagram with the labels "SUN", "SEA", "CLOUD", and "RAIN" and curved arrows between them. Lily, Mia, Leo, and May sit at their desks with notebooks.
- inline-para-2: Teacher Kim holds a small pot of steaming water on a table and a cold metal plate above it; drops of water hang under the plate. Mia and Leo stand a safe step back and watch.
- inline-para-3: Lily's blog page headed "OUR SCIENCE PAGE" with three headings, "The sun heats the sea", "Clouds are made", and "Rain falls", each beside a small drawing (a sun over waves, a cloud, and rain over a river).

### L05 The Frog Under the Shell

Text type: story: a traditional tale told by Grandpa. Genre: Pets & Animals. App type: fiction. Place: Green Hill, the grandparents' village.

On Sunday, February 7, Dad takes Lily, Tom, and Pip to Grandma and Grandpa's house at Green Hill. After lunch Lily says, "I know everything about our school," and Grandpa asks, "Do you know the frog under the coconut shell?" Then he tells "The Frog Under the Shell": a small frog lives under an old coconut shell next to a deep pond and believes that the shell is the whole sky. A duck dives into the pond and says, "The sky is wide, and no one has seen all of it," but the frog does not believe the duck. At last the frog climbs on top of the shell and looks up at the wide blue sky. Lily says nothing, but that evening she takes the atlas from Grandpa's shelf, and Tom sees it.

- hero: The porch of a wooden house in the afternoon. Grandpa sits on a chair and tells the story with his hands. Lily and Tom sit on the steps; Pip lies on the porch with the red ball. A half coconut shell stands upside down on the porch rail.

### L07 Five Questions for Nurse Jill

Text type: functional: an interview with five questions, for the class blog. Genre: Health & Body. App type: nonfiction. Place: the school nurse's room (the interview) and May's post on the blog.

On Wednesday, February 10, May posts the interview "Five Questions for Nurse Jill" on the Explorers' Blog; she asked the questions in the nurse's room on Tuesday, after lunch. Nurse Jill says that she sees about fifteen children every day and that headaches and tired children are the most common problems. She says that children need at least nine hours of sleep, because the brain needs a rest, and that fast food and sweet drinks give energy for only a short time. She also tells readers to wash their hands, to drink water, and to tell a teacher at once when a friend feels ill.
