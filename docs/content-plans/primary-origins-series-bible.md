# Primary Advantage Origins — Series Bible

Version 1.2 | Date 2026-10-01 | Status: Spelling and names approved 2026-10-01 | Owner: Daniel Bo | Internal

This file fixes the story world for every new Primary Advantage Origins text: Origins 3.2, the Origins 3.1 lesson-12 replacement, Origins 1, and online-only articles at levels 1–3. Writers, reviewers, illustrators, and AI prompts use it. The printed books Origins 2 and Origins 3.1 are frozen. This file does not change them. It sets the rules from now on.

Source: an analysis of the 28 printed lesson texts (`primary/origins-2-a0/`, `primary/origins-3.1-a0/`), 2026-09-30.

## 1. What the printed books already show

- Pip the puppy appears in 25 of 28 lessons. Six or seven children rotate around him.
- Every article starts "This is [name]. [Name] is a [role]." Every article ends with a feeling or a value ("Pip is happy.", "Teamwork is good.").
- The red ball appears in 9 lessons. It is the strongest recurring object.
- All 28 texts are fiction. No text is a sign, a notice, a timetable, a card, or a set of instructions.
- The books disagree with themselves. Pip is brown in 5 lessons, yellow in 1, and white in 1. Pip belongs to Lily (O2.7), to Mia (O3.1.5), and to Tom (O3.1.10). In Origins 2, "Mom" and "Dad" are Pip's dog parents. "Mom", "mum", and "Mama" all occur. Ben is a cousin, a friend, and a farmer.

## 2. Canon from now on

### Pip

| Fact | Rule |
|---|---|
| Species, size | A small puppy. He stays a puppy in every book. |
| Colour | Brown. He wears a red collar. (Brown is the majority colour in print.) |
| Pronoun | He. |
| Home | Pip lives with Tom and Lily and their mom and dad, in a house near the park. |
| Speech | Pip says "Woof!" He can think in simple sentences in a story ("Pip wants the ball."). He does not hold conversations. |
| Favourite thing | His red ball. |
| Character | Curious, happy, sometimes afraid, always kind. He learns something small in each story. |

Do not write that Pip's parents are dogs named Mom and Dad. Do not give Pip a new owner. Do not tell a "how Pip came home" story. It would conflict with Origins 3.1 lesson 5 ("Mia and Pip").

## 3. Language rules

- **Spelling:** American English, as in the printed books ("Mom", "colors", "favorite"). The Cambridge lists give US forms as variants.
- **Opening:** Stories may keep "This is [name]." Functional texts (signs, cards, lists, instructions, chants) open in their own form. From Origins 3.2, vary the opening. Do not use the same first line in every lesson.
- **Closing:** Keep one short line with a feeling or a value.
- **Sentences:** Short and declarative. Level 3 adds questions ("Where is Pip?"), "can" and "can't", "There is / There are", "has got", prepositions of place, and short imperatives ("Look!", "Don't run!"). These are all on the Cambridge Starters grammar list.
- **Question marks:** Put at least two real questions in most texts. Origins 2 and Origins 3.1 have one question mark in 28 texts.
- **Dialogue:** Use double quotation marks. One speaker per paragraph line.
- **Numbers:** Write numbers as words ("nine", "twelve"). Times are full hours ("seven o'clock").
- **Colour tags:** Keep one colour word on important nouns. It helps the youngest readers. Do not colour every noun.
- **Vocabulary:** Glossed words come from the Cambridge Starters list first. See the lesson plan for each book.

## 4. Values and safety

- Keep the values the books already teach: sharing, kindness, bravery, fair play, helping, teamwork.
- No tests, prizes, rankings, or winners and losers as the point of a story.
- No dangerous acts shown as normal (a child playing in traffic). Everyday scenes are fine, for example a nine-year-old who walks the dog to the shop.
- Thai home life is the default: shoes off at the door, rice at dinner, a market trip, a school morning line-up.
- No brand names, no real people, no religious holidays shown as a family's own practice. "My Happy Holiday" (Origins 3.1) stays as printed. New texts use birthdays and school events for celebrations.

## 5. Illustration notes

- Three images per lesson (the lesson JSON field `article_image_url`).
- Pip: small, brown, red collar, big eyes. Same look in every image.
- Children: the character sheets fix each face, hair, and outfit. School scenes: Thai school uniform.
- Signs and notices (Origins 3.2): the sign text in the picture is the same English text as in the article, letter for letter. Large, clear capital and lower-case letters.
- Character sheets: one image per character (Pip, Tom, Lily, Mia, Ben, Leo, Sam, May, Pat, Teacher Kim, Mom, Dad, Grandma, Grandpa, Squeaky), made with `mmx image` and approved by Daniel. Every lesson image job passes the sheet of its main character as `--subject-ref type=character,image=<sheet>`. The sheets live in `docs/content-plans/character-sheets/`.
