## 1. Summary

Adventure 9.3 is a book of level 9 (A2+, `cefr_level = 'A2'`, `ra_level = 9`). It has 14 workbook lessons. Each lesson has 12 glossed words, 2–4 target objectives, and one main grammar point of level 9. QR key: `a9.3/<lesson>`. The book is the school holiday before M1: Thursday, April 1 to Friday, May 14, 2027. It has a holiday cooking course with Chef Lucy, family days (a visit to Green Hill, a bus ride to a concert), a summer job at home, the holiday blog, the pen pals' holiday emails, and the last week before M1 (an open morning in the M1 building, a list, Tom's advice, a new uniform). The Explorers club has ended; the friends still meet. M1 starts on Monday, May 17, and no lesson shows that day.

Text mix: 6 stories and 8 informational or functional texts. The stories are in L02, L04, L07, L10, L13, L14; the other lessons are informational or functional. The informational types are a program with a key (L01), instructions (L03), an interview (L05), a news report (L06), a school science text with headings (L08), an email with a reply (L09), a talk about a person of the past (L11), and two reviews of one event (L12). The book has a traditional story in L07 (*The Peacock and the Crane*, a tale that Grandma tells at Green Hill). The stories ask the reader to infer feelings and facts that the text does not state. Dates are written in one form, month first (*April 1*; in the text, *April first*). Every weekday agrees with the 2027 calendar (April 1, 2027 is a Thursday). No lesson falls on a public holiday: Chakri Day (Tuesday, April 6), the water days (Tuesday, April 13 to Thursday, April 15), Labor Day (Saturday, May 1), and Coronation Day (Tuesday, May 4) have no lesson. The book has no club trip (the club has ended). Places: the town library, the community center kitchen, the town square, Green Hill, the town bus and the park, and the M1 building.

Level 9 uses the A2 Key list on top of the grammar of levels 5–8. The new grammar of level 9 comes in here: the passive (present and past simple, common verbs, no agent), reporting with *say / tell + that*, the sequence and cohesion words (*first, next, then, after that, finally; so, because*), *may* and *might* for possibility, and (very rare) the present perfect passive with *just / already / yet*. Each lesson has one main point (section 6). Voice follows bible §7: a close third person in the past simple for stories; a named writer for a blog post, a diary entry, or an email (*Posted by Leo*, *Dear Tom, ... Best wishes, Alex*); headings and no "I" in an informational text of the school or the library (L08, L11); a program, a card, or a news report speaks to the reader. The stories show a feeling with an action or a face, never with the word for it (the word may be in speech).

### L07 The Peacock and the Crane

Text type: story: a traditional tale told by Grandma. Genre: Animals & Nature. App type: fiction. Place: Green Hill, the grandparents' village.

On Saturday, April 17, Dad takes Lily and Tom (and Pip) to Green Hill. Grandma smiles and says, "Today you will hear the story of the peacock and the crane." One day, a gray crane, a foreign bird that flies across the wide ocean every year, stops by the garden pond, and the peacock laughs at her: "What a pity! Your feathers have no color at all." The crane answers, "Your tail is beautiful, but I can fly over the hills and see the whole world. You can only walk in this garden," and she opens her wings and flies away. Tom says, "The peacock seems very quiet now," Lily asks, "Was the crane angry?", and Grandma only says, "Maybe. What do you think?"
