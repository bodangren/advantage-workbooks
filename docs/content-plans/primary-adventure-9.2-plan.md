## 1. Summary

Adventure 9.2 is a book of level 9 (A2+, `cefr_level = 'A2'`, `ra_level = 9`). It has 14 workbook lessons. Each lesson has 12 glossed words, 3–4 target objectives, and one main grammar point of level 9. QR key: `a9.2/<lesson>`. The book is the last month of Lily's year in Primary 6: the final tests and how to study, the one video call with the pen-pal class, the last club trip, the last Explorers meeting, Farewell Day, and the last pen-pal emails. It asks the reader to infer feelings and facts that the text does not state (R40.1, R40.5, L39.3), to find who a text is for (R40.7), and to read news, an interview, an opinion, a review, and a school-subject text.

Text mix: 6 stories and 8 informational or functional texts. The stories are in L02, L04, L05, L07, L09, L11; the other lessons are informational or functional: a program with a key and a leaflet (L01), a talk with steps (L03), a school-subject text with headings (L06), a short news report (L08), an interview (L10), an opinion with two ads (L12), a review (L13), and two texts on one topic (L14). The book has a traditional story in L04 (*The Ant and the Grasshopper*, a fable that Mom tells on the veranda). Dates run from Monday, March 1 to Monday, March 29, written in one form, month first (*March 1*; in the text, *March first*). Every weekday agrees with the 2027 calendar (March 1, 2027 is a Monday). No lesson falls on a public holiday. The last school day of Primary 6 is Friday, March 19; the school holiday starts on Saturday, March 20. The only club trip of the book is the small zoo (Saturday, March 13, L09).

Level 9 uses the A2 Key list on top of the grammar of levels 5–8. The new A2 Key grammar comes in here: the passive (present and past simple, no agent), reporting with *say / tell + that*, sequence and cohesion words (*first, next, then, after that, finally, however, so, because*), *may / might* for possibility, and, very rarely, the present perfect passive with *just, already, yet*. Each lesson has one main point (section 6). Voice follows bible §7: a close third person in the past simple for stories; a named writer for a blog post, a diary entry, or an email (*Posted by Lily*, *Dear Lily, ... Best wishes, Grace*); headings and no "I" in an informational text of the club or the school; a leaflet, a program, a poster, or a talk speaks to the reader. In a story, the narrator names no feeling that the brief marks as "not stated"; an action, a face, or a short line of speech shows it, and the closing line stays calm. The linking words *and, so, but,* and *because* come in every lesson.

### L06 Leo's Revision Notes: Mixing and Changing

Text type: functional: a school science text with headings, on the class blog. Genre: Science & Space. App type: nonfiction. Place: Leo's desk at home.

On Monday, March 8, after the English test, Leo writes his revision notes for Wednesday's science test, and Teacher Kim puts them on the blog ("Posted by Leo"). The notes have three headings: "What is a mixture?", "Changes you can undo", and "Changes you cannot undo". Leo writes that chemistry is the science of materials and how they change, that salt mixed with water is a mixture, and that perfume is a mixture of oil, water, and flower scents. For his experiment he puts ice in a cup with a digital thermometer: the ice changes to water and can change back to ice, but bread that is grilled becomes toast and cannot change back. Leo asks, "Can you get the salt back?" and answers, "Yes, if you heat the water." He ends with a joke about his room, which is a mixture that he cannot undo before Wednesday.

- hero: Leo sits at a desk at home. In front of him lie an open notebook headed "MIXING AND CHANGING", a glass of water, a small bowl of salt, and a spoon. His laptop shows a blog page with the line "Posted by Leo".
