## 1. What carries over from the Origins canon

- **Pip.** A small brown puppy with a red collar. He says "Woof!" and does not talk. He lives with Tom and Lily. His favorite thing is the red ball. He stays a puppy in every book.
- **Home people.** Tom, Lily, Mom, Dad, Grandma, and Grandpa. Spell "Mom" and "Dad".
- **Friends.** Mia (Lily's best friend, next door), Ben (Tom's friend), Leo (Lily's classmate), Sam (neighbor), May and Pat (a sister and brother who live in the white house next to the school), Teacher Kim, and Squeaky the mouse.
- **Quest 4 additions.** Aunt Sue is Mom's sister. Uncle Dan is Dad's brother. Snow is Mia's white kitten.
- **Fixed facts.** Tom likes football and his bike. Lily likes books and drawing. Mia loves animals. Leo is funny. May likes English. Pat is small and brave. Ben is only Tom's friend. There is no "Farmer Ben".
- **Places.** Home, May's house, the park (pond, grass, trees, playground), school, the market, Grandma and Grandpa's house, and the street. Places have plain nouns, not proper names. The Thai home habits stay: shoes off at the door, rice at dinner, the morning line-up.
- **Language.** American spelling. Short sentences. Double quotation marks for dialogue. One closing line with a feeling or a value.
- **Values and safety.** Sharing, kindness, teamwork, and fair play. No winners and losers as the point of a story. No brand names. No real people. No religious holidays as a family's own practice.
- **Cast sheets.** Every child and adult in the Origins bible keeps the sheet in `docs/content-plans/character-sheets/`.

What changes: "All people are Thai" is true for Quest 5–6 and for the home world of Adventure. From Adventure 7, the pen-pal class (section 4) brings people who are not Thai. Daniel approved this on 2026-10-06. The Origins rule on image prompts does not change (section 6).

Rules:

- May is one year older than her classmates Lily, Mia, and Leo. She is in their class, as in Quest 4. Do not explain it.
- Lily's class is Teacher Kim's class in every book. Teacher Kim stays with the class from Quest 5 to Adventure 9.
- Quest 5 is one school year after Quest 4. Quest 6.1 and 6.2 are the next school year. They share the ages.
- Adventure 7, 8, and 9 are one school year: Lily's last year of primary school (P6). Adventure 7 is the first term, Adventure 8 is the middle, and Adventure 9 is the end. Nobody has a birthday in Adventure 7–9. This keeps each age fixed in a book, and the books show growth through skills, not age.
- Pip is a puppy in every book. Never write that he is "old" or "big". Do not give his age.
- The cast sheets stay the same. Show age by height only: Tom and Ben are taller than Lily and Mia. Write no new look for an older child. The picture style shows the older child by size and pose. If a sheet looks too young for a 13-year-old in a test image, Daniel decides on a new sheet (question 2 in the report).
- Use the school uniform of the Origins books for every child: a white shirt and dark blue shorts or skirt. Tom and Ben wear the same.

### Pip in Adventure

- **How often.** Pip appears in about one lesson in three: 4 or 5 of the 14 workbook lessons in a book. Bank articles follow the same share.
- **Where.** Home stories, the park, the walk to school, and the club photos. Pip does not go on a school trip. He may come to the nature park or the park for a picnic when the story says Dad or Tom brings him.
- **What he does.** He finds something, he is in a photo, he is in a cartoon of Leo's, or he spoils a plan in a funny way. He is never the narrator. He does not read emails.
- **What he does not do.** He does not talk, he does not travel abroad, he does not meet the pen pals in person, and he is not in a diary or a news text as a person. A pen pal may ask about him in an email ("How is Pip?").
- **Voice.** The narrator may say what Pip wants in a simple sentence ("Pip wants the ball."). This is the Origins rule.

## 5. Names and spelling rules for new characters

- **Thai people in Thailand.** Use an English-style nickname from the Cambridge Starters name list (the Origins rule). Do not use a name that the Origins or Quest cast already has.
- **One name, one person.** No two characters in the Origins, Quest, and Adventure books have the same first name.
- **Names used in this file:** Alex, Nick, Matt, Mark, Ann, Lucy, and Jill are all on the Starters name list. Grace is too. Ravi, Nadia, and Ong are not.
- **Pen pals.** The names are English names or a common first name in Singapore. Use first names only. Teacher: "Ms. Ong", with a period after "Ms" (American usage).
- **Titles.** "Teacher" for a school teacher of the school (Teacher Kim, Teacher Nick). "Ranger", "Guide", "Chef", "Coach", and "Nurse" for a job title. Use "Ms." for a teacher at the pen-pal school.
- **Spelling.** American English: "color", "favorite", "mom", "neighbor", "gray", "theater", "program". Write "football", not "soccer" (the Origins word). Use "sneakers", "pants" or "trousers" as in Quest 4 (keep "trousers" for adults). Dates in text: "October 5" or "5 October" in Adventure. Use one form in a book.
- **Place words.** "Singapore" and "Thailand" are proper names. Put them in `allow`. Do not name a Thai city.
- **Numbers.** Numbers as words up to one hundred in Quest. From Adventure, a number in a timetable, price, date, or list may be a figure (a text type rule). Say so in the lesson file when a text uses figures.
- **No new family names.** Do not add last names for the main cast.
- **No real people, schools, or brands.** Palm Lake Primary School is invented.

## 7. Voice rules for Adventure

### What stays the same from Quest

- Short, direct sentences. Active voice. One idea in a sentence.
- Dialogue in double quotation marks. One speaker for each line.
- At least two real questions in most texts.
- A closing line with a feeling or a value.
- Warm, kind, calm. No sarcasm. No fear that a child cannot handle.
- Pip is "he". Thai home life is the default.

### Dialogue

- Children speak as 11-year-olds speak in a school: short, with "Let's", "Shall we", "Why?", "Because", and "I think". Use contractions in speech ("I'm", "we're", "don't").
- One speech act in each line. Use "says" or "asks" as the tag. Use few other tags.
- Adults speak in clear steps. The ranger and the guide give facts. The teacher gives a task and a kind comment.
- Dialogue gives the A2 conversation targets: a school talk, an excuse, a phone message, a call, agreement and disagreement. Give each speaker one voice in the audio (plan D5).

### The narrator

- The narrator is a close third person in the past simple, as in Adventure 7 grammar (plan §7). From Adventure 8 the story may use the present perfect in speech.
- The narrator names a place or a person at first mention. It does not explain jokes. It does not teach in the story ("Lily learned that …"). The value stays in the closing line.
- The narrator tells what a character sees, says, and does. It tells a feeling with an action or a face.
- An informational text has headings from Adventure 7. The writer is the club or the school. It does not use "I".

### First-person blog or diary voice

- A blog post or a diary entry has one named writer: "Posted by Lily" or "Dear diary".
- The writer says "I" and "we". The writer talks to the reader ("Do you like fish?"). The writer gives a date, a place, and the order of events (first, then, after that, finally).
- A diary entry is for the writer. It may name a feeling ("I was nervous"). A blog post is for the public. It gives facts and an opinion with a reason ("I liked the park because …").
- An email has an opening ("Dear Grace,") and a closing ("Best wishes, May"). It has one purpose, such as a question, a plan, or thanks. Use the A2 email rules in plan §3.
- Each child has a small habit in the first person. Lily is careful and notes details. Mia loves animals and writes about them first. Leo makes a joke and then a fact. May asks questions. Grace asks about Lily's books. Ravi writes recipes. Alex writes about robots and games. Nadia writes about animals.
- The blog and the emails use the child's own level. Do not make an 11-year-old write like an adult.

### Vocabulary and grammar in voice

- Adventure texts follow the profiles `adventure-7`, `adventure-8`, and `adventure-9` (plan §5) and the grammar file [`data/grammar-levels-5-9.md`](data/grammar-levels-5-9.md).
- The linking words "and", "so", "but", and "because" come in Adventure 8.1. Use them often in the voice of the blog.
