# Preface

## Welcome

This manual is for the current **primary** Reading Advantage workbook format used in projects such as `origins-2-a0` and `origins-3.1-a0`.

It is written to match the **actual primary workbook sequence** generated by the workbook system, not the older secondary teacher guide flow.

---

## What This Guide Covers

The primary workbook is a **13-step lesson**:

1. Before You Read
2. Key Vocabulary
3. Read the Article
4. Collect Vocabulary
5. Deep Reading Notes
6. Collect Sentences
7. Comprehension Check
8. Guided Response
9. Vocabulary Practice
10. Sentence Practice
11. Guided Writing
12. Language Questions
13. Lesson Reflection

This guide uses those same step names so teachers can follow the workbook page by page.

---

## Who This Manual Is For

This manual is designed for:

* Primary classroom teachers using the Reading Advantage workbook with a whole class
* Coordinators and coaches supporting primary implementation
* New teachers who need a dependable routine that matches the printed workbook

---

## Key Principle

In the primary workbook, the teacher should **anchor instruction to the printed page** and use the app to support reading, audio, vocabulary review, sentence review, and later writing feedback.

The workbook is not a note sheet for a separate lesson. The workbook **is the lesson path**.

---

## How This Manual Is Organized

This manual contains four sections:

### 1. Quick Reference

A short live-teaching script for teachers who already know the flow.

### 2. Complete Teacher-Led Lesson Plan

A full walkthrough of one primary lesson from start to finish.

### 3. Step-by-Step Guide

Detailed notes for each of the 13 workbook steps.

### 4. Trainer's Guide

Implementation notes for coaches, heads of department, and mentors.

## Primary-Specific Differences From Older Guides

This primary version differs from the older secondary guide in several important ways:

* The workbook ends at **Step 13**, not Step 14 plus a separate final reflection
* Reflection and homework are built directly into the printed lesson ending
* Writing is **Step 11: Guided Writing**, not a later Step 13 writing block
* Language Questions is **Step 12**
* The student sees workbook labels such as **Deep Reading Notes**, **Guided Response**, and **Guided Writing**
* The printed workbook currently gives students:
  * 4 vocabulary collection boxes
  * 2 sentence collection boxes
  * 4 comprehension questions in the Origins primary series

Teachers should be trained to follow the printed workbook structure the student is holding.

---

## Core Teacher Rule

> Match your language to the workbook page.
> Model the task clearly.
> Use the app to support the workbook, not to replace it.
# Quick Reference: Primary Lesson Script

> **Purpose:** This page is a live-teaching memory aid for the current primary workbook format.

---

## Phase 1: Launch the Lesson

### Step 1 - Before You Read

* Say: *"Look at the title and picture. Show your interest with the stars."*
* Do: Point to title, image, and interest rating
* Students: Mark stars and respond to the prompt if used

### Step 2 - Key Vocabulary

* Say: *"Listen. Repeat. Then write or draw to help you remember."*
* Do: Model pronunciation and meaning
* Students: Repeat and use the second column for translation, drawing, or a simple sentence

### Step 3 - Read the Article

* Say: *"Listen and follow first. Then we will read more carefully."*
* Do: Use the app QR/audio support and project the article
* Students: Track paragraphs and mark new words, confusing parts, interesting parts, and save-worthy sentences

---

## Phase 2: Return to the Text

### Step 4 - Collect Vocabulary

* Say: *"Write important new words from the article."*
* Do: Model choosing a useful word and its paragraph number
* Students: Fill the 4 word boxes

### Step 5 - Deep Reading Notes

* Say: *"Read again carefully. Write the main idea of each paragraph."*
* Do: Re-read slowly and pause by paragraph
* Students: Write one main idea per paragraph

### Step 6 - Collect Sentences

* Say: *"Choose 2 sentences you want to study."*
* Do: Model how to choose a sentence and why
* Students: Copy 2 sentences and check why they chose them

---

## Phase 3: Check Understanding

### Step 7 - Comprehension Check

* Routine: Think -> partner -> evidence -> answer
* Do: Discuss each multiple-choice question and require a clue from the article
* Students: Choose, justify, and record score

### Step 8 - Guided Response

* Say: *"Answer in complete sentences. Use the article."*
* Do: Model the question, hint, sentence starters, and guided frame
* Students: Write a short response and self-check

---

## Phase 4: Practice and Produce

### Step 9 - Vocabulary Practice

* Do: Complete matching and fill-in-the-blank work
* Students: Use the workbook and word bank carefully

### Step 10 - Sentence Practice

* Do: Model one item if needed
* Students: Put words in order and complete sentence prompts

### Step 11 - Guided Writing

* Say: *"Plan first. Then draft."*
* Do: Model the planner, sentence frames, and draft expectations
* Students: Plan, write, and self-check

### Step 12 - Language Questions

* Rule: **Write first, AI second**
* Do: Collect a few student questions and answer them with teacher-mediated AI support
* Students: Write questions and record answers

### Step 13 - Lesson Reflection

* Do: Finish with reflection, understanding check, effort rating, and homework reminder
* Students: Reflect honestly and note next steps

---

> **Primary lesson rule:** Keep the workbook page and the teacher talk in sync.
# Reading Advantage Primary

## Complete Teacher-Led Lesson Plan

**Audience:** Primary EFL/ESL learners using the printed primary workbook  
**Workbook model:** Current 13-step primary format  
**Typical duration:** 55-75 minutes  
**Reference projects:** `origins-2-a0`, `origins-3.1-a0`

---

## Core Goal

The goal of the lesson is to help students **use the workbook and app together in a stable routine**:

* read with support
* notice vocabulary and sentences
* show understanding with evidence
* practice language in the workbook
* write with planning
* reflect before leaving

The teacher should make the lesson feel predictable and page-based. Students should always know **which workbook step they are in**.

---

## Before Class

Prepare:

* the workbook page sequence for the lesson
* the article in the app
* audio playback
* projector or large screen
* writing tools

Check:

* the article QR path works
* audio is clear
* the teacher can move between article, questions, and any follow-up app screens smoothly

---

# Phase 1: Launch the Lesson

## Step 1: Before You Read

Project the lesson title and image. Ask students to look first, not read everything at once. Have them mark their interest with the stars. If the workbook includes a thinking prompt, give brief talk time or a short written response.

Teacher move:

> "Show me your interest first. There is no wrong answer."

Purpose:

* opens attention
* lowers pressure
* helps students enter the topic before full reading

## Step 2: Key Vocabulary

Move word by word. Say the word, play or model pronunciation, and confirm meaning simply. Students should use the second column to write a translation, make a drawing, or write a simple sentence.

Teacher move:

> "Say it with me. Now show the meaning in your own way."

Purpose:

* reduces friction before reading
* makes the vocabulary column active, not decorative

## Step 3: Read the Article

Use the QR/app support and project the article. During the first pass, keep the flow smooth. During the second pass, encourage students to mark:

* new words
* confusing parts
* interesting parts
* sentences worth saving

Teacher move:

> "First we follow the story. Later we stop and notice details."

Purpose:

* gives students a full-text experience
* prepares them for later collection work

---

# Phase 2: Return to the Text

## Step 4: Collect Vocabulary

Students now transfer key new words from the article into the workbook. The printed page currently provides **4 collection boxes**, so the teacher should not require more than the page supports during class.

Teacher move:

> "Choose the words that are important for you, then write the paragraph number."

## Step 5: Deep Reading Notes

Read paragraph by paragraph. Students write one main idea for each paragraph. In the current Origins primary lessons, this is a direct paragraph-summary routine, so keep it short and concrete.

Teacher move:

> "What is this paragraph mostly about?"

## Step 6: Collect Sentences

Students choose **2** sentences from the article and say why they chose them. The workbook gives checkboxes for grammar, vocabulary, and useful phrase. Use those labels in teacher talk.

Teacher move:

> "Choose two sentences that will help your English later."

Purpose of Steps 4-6:

* move students from reading to noticing
* build habits of selecting language, not just answering questions

---

# Phase 3: Check Understanding

## Step 7: Comprehension Check

The current Origins primary lessons contain **4 multiple-choice questions**. Read each question, let students think, then discuss evidence before they mark answers. Students should also write one clue from the article.

Teacher move:

> "Which part of the article helps you know the answer?"

Do not rush straight to correctness. The clue line matters.

## Step 8: Guided Response

This is the workbook's short written response step. Use the printed hint if present, the sentence starters, and the guided frame. The goal is not a perfect essay. The goal is a supported, text-based response in complete sentences.

Teacher move:

> "Use the article. Use the frame. Write a real answer, not one word."

Purpose:

* connects comprehension to writing
* normalizes supported sentence production

---

# Phase 4: Practice and Produce

## Step 9: Vocabulary Practice

Students complete:

* **A. Match the Words**
* **B. Fill in the Blanks**

Keep the work brisk. This is not the moment for long explanations.

## Step 10: Sentence Practice

Students complete:

* **C. Put the Words in Order**
* **D. Complete the Sentence**

Model one item if needed, then release students to work.

## Step 11: Guided Writing

This is a major primary workbook step and should be taught exactly as printed:

1. Read the prompt
2. Use the planner
3. Use the sentence frames
4. Draft
5. Self-check

If a writing QR code is available in future primary lessons, it should be framed as **after planning and drafting**, never before.

Teacher move:

> "The planner comes first. The draft comes second."

## Step 12: Language Questions

Students write language questions first. Then the teacher selects a few and uses AI support carefully. This must stay teacher-mediated.

Teacher move:

> "Write the question clearly first. Then we can ask for help."

## Step 13: Lesson Reflection

Close the lesson with:

* the reflection prompt
* understanding rating
* effort rating
* interest/follow-up prompts
* homework reminder

The current workbook already includes homework directly after reflection, so teachers should end on that page rather than inventing a separate ending routine.

Teacher move:

> "Finish the reflection before you pack up. This is part of the lesson."

---

## Final Teacher Reminder

For the primary workbook, consistency matters more than novelty. Students should experience the lesson as:

* one stable workbook path
* supported by the app
* explained with simple teacher language
* completed step by step in order
# Step 1: Before You Read

Use the title, image, and interest stars to start the lesson calmly.

Teacher actions:

* point to the title and image
* ask students to look before discussing
* direct students to mark their interest

Teacher language:

> "Look first."
>
> "How interested are you in this topic?"

What students do:

* mark the stars
* respond to the optional prompt if present

Watch for:

* students waiting instead of writing
* students trying to jump ahead
# Step 2: Key Vocabulary

This step should match the printed vocabulary table.

Teacher actions:

* pronounce each word clearly
* confirm meaning simply
* let students repeat
* direct students to use the second column

Teacher language:

> "Say it."
>
> "Now show the meaning in your notebook space."

Students may:

* write a translation
* draw a picture
* write a very short sentence

Do not:

* over-explain every word
* turn the step into a long oral quiz
# Step 3: Read the Article

This is the entry point to the text and the app-supported reading experience.

Teacher actions:

* project the article
* use app audio if available
* keep the first pass smooth
* use the printed marking legend on the workbook page

Students should mark:

* new word
* confusing part
* interesting part
* save this sentence

Teacher language:

> "First, follow the article."
>
> "Later, we will use our marks."

Do not stop too often during the first pass.
# Step 4: Collect Vocabulary

This step uses the student's own article markings.

Teacher actions:

* model one useful word choice
* show how to write the paragraph number
* keep the class within the printed 4-box limit

Teacher language:

> "Choose important words from your reading."

Students do:

* copy up to 4 useful words
* record the paragraph number for each

Important note:

The current primary workbook page supports **4 collected words in class**. Do not teach this as a 5-word in-class task.
# Step 5: Deep Reading Notes

This step is paragraph-by-paragraph meaning work.

Teacher actions:

* re-read slowly
* pause after each paragraph
* ask for the main idea in simple language

Teacher language:

> "What is this paragraph mostly about?"

Students do:

* read again
* write one main idea for each paragraph

Keep answers short and clear. This is not a full summary paragraph.
# Step 6: Collect Sentences

This step teaches students to notice useful language, not just words.

Teacher actions:

* model one sentence choice
* explain the printed reason labels:
  * grammar
  * vocabulary
  * useful phrase

Teacher language:

> "Choose two sentences that can help your English."

Students do:

* copy 2 sentences
* check why they chose each one

Important note:

The workbook currently gives **2 sentence slots**. Teach to the page the student has.
# Step 7: Comprehension Check

In the current Origins primary workbooks, this step contains **4 multiple-choice questions**.

Teacher actions:

* read the question
* give think time
* ask for partner talk
* require a clue from the article

Teacher language:

> "Which clue helped you?"

Students do:

* choose one answer
* write a clue line
* record score

Important note:

The workbook flow should match the printed question count. Do not talk about a 5-question score routine when the page contains 4 questions.
# Step 8: Guided Response

This is the primary workbook's supported short writing step.

Teacher actions:

* read the question aloud
* point out the hint if present
* point out sentence starters
* point out the guided frame
* remind students to self-check

Teacher language:

> "Use the article."
>
> "Write in complete sentences."

Students do:

* answer the question
* support the answer with article information
* complete the self-check
# Step 9: Vocabulary Practice

This step is workbook practice, not a new explanation block.

Students complete:

* A. Match the Words
* B. Fill in the Blanks

Teacher actions:

* model one item if needed
* keep the pace steady
* use the word bank

Teacher language:

> "Use the word bank carefully."

Avoid turning this into long vocabulary teaching. It is practice and review.
# Step 10: Sentence Practice

This step focuses on sentence control.

Students complete:

* C. Put the Words in Order
* D. Complete the Sentence

Teacher actions:

* model one ordering item if necessary
* remind students to write full sentences

Teacher language:

> "Make a real sentence, not just words."

Use correction briefly and clearly. Do not expand into a grammar lecture.
# Step 11: Guided Writing

This step must follow the printed workbook order:

1. writing prompt
2. plan your writing
3. sentence starters or frames
4. write your draft
5. self-check

Teacher actions:

* read the prompt
* model one planner response
* insist on planning before drafting
* set a clear quiet-writing routine

Teacher language:

> "Plan first."
>
> "Then write your draft."

If the lesson includes writing QR support, frame it as:

> "After the draft, you can type it in the app for feedback."

Never let app typing replace planning.
# Step 12: Language Questions

This step is teacher-mediated.

Teacher actions:

* have students write questions first
* select a few strong questions
* use AI support only after the question is written
* help students record the answer

Teacher language:

> "Write the question clearly first."

Students do:

* write one or two language questions
* listen to the teacher-mediated answer
* record the answer in the workbook

Rule:

**Workbook first, AI second.**
# Step 13: Lesson Reflection

This is the actual ending of the current primary workbook lesson.

Teacher actions:

* direct students to complete the reflection page
* use the printed understanding scale
* use the printed effort rating
* point out the homework box before dismissal

Teacher language:

> "Finish the reflection before you pack up."

Students do:

* complete the reflection prompts
* rate understanding
* rate effort
* note homework

Important note:

Do not teach this as a separate unnumbered ending. In the current primary workbook, reflection is **Step 13**.
# Trainer's Guide: Primary Workbook Implementation

## Trainer Goal

Train teachers to stay aligned with the **printed primary workbook sequence**.

The biggest implementation risk is drift from older secondary routines or app-first habits.

---

## Non-Negotiables

1. Use the **primary step names** the student sees.
2. Teach the current lesson as a **13-step workbook flow**.
3. Keep collection tasks aligned to the page:
   * 4 vocabulary boxes
   * 2 sentence boxes
4. Keep writing **planner first, draft second, app feedback later**.
5. Keep Language Questions **teacher-mediated**.
6. End on **Step 13: Lesson Reflection**, including homework.

---

## What Trainers Should Listen For

Good teacher language:

* "Open to Step 5: Deep Reading Notes."
* "Now go to Step 8: Guided Response."
* "Finish Step 13 before you pack up."

Risk language:

* "Now we are on Step 14."
* "Save 5 words here."
* "Save 5 sentences here."
* "Skip the planner and type in the app."

---

## Coaching Focus By Phase

### Phase 1

Make sure the teacher launches the page clearly and does not over-explain vocabulary before reading.

### Phase 2

Make sure the teacher returns to the text and uses paragraph main ideas, collected words, and collected sentences exactly as printed.

### Phase 3

Make sure the teacher requires article evidence and complete-sentence thinking.

### Phase 4

Make sure the teacher treats workbook practice as practice, not lecture.

### Ending

Make sure the teacher finishes with reflection and homework on the printed final page.

---

## Final Trainer Message

> In the primary workbook, clarity beats creativity.
> If the teacher stays synchronized with the page, the lesson becomes much easier for students to follow.
